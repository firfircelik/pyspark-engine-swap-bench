# Update (Nov 2026): Polars

This update adds a Polars 2.2.0 line to the same Phase 2 protocol used for DuckDB, Sail, Comet and Spark in the original study. The runner is the merged script from [PR #1](https://github.com/firfircelik/pyspark-engine-swap-bench/pull/1) by Ritchie Vink, adjusted in commit 04eaba5 to run from its own `venv-polars` so it cannot change the Sail or DuckDB environment. Polars executes the same TPC-H SQL text and the same six ETL statements through `pl.SQLContext` on the streaming engine. Numbers below are from a two-rep matrix run on 27 November 2026 (17:11-17:41 Europe/Istanbul) on the original study box, under the same 9 GiB cgroup cap. The job set matches the published DuckDB line: local files, MinIO loopback, and MinIO behind toxiproxy at 0 ms and 10 ms of added latency for TPC-H Q1-Q22; loopback and 10 ms for ETL E1-E6. Rep 3 was not run, because the published Phase 2 totals already dropped it. SF100 was not run. Medians are over the two complete reps, the same method as the published tables. Wall time is the sum of per-query `wall_s`, not process elapsed. Raw JSONL, per-query hashes, ETL digests and the E6 probe are in [`results/polars/`](https://github.com/firfircelik/pyspark-engine-swap-bench/tree/main/results/polars).

## Speed

At SF30, Polars sits near DuckDB and Sail, and well ahead of Comet and Spark. On local Parquet, the median TPC-H total was 60.975 s for Polars, 48.412 s for DuckDB, 49.584 s for Sail without join reorder, and 52.796 s with reorder. Comet 4.2.0 default was 237.253 s; Spark 4.2.0 default was 386.568 s. On MinIO loopback the gap to DuckDB is similar: Polars 72.550 s, DuckDB 55.330 s, Sail with reorder 71.980 s. At 10 ms of added object-store latency the order changes. Polars 86.597 s is faster than DuckDB 98.582 s and Sail (121-122 s), and much faster than Comet (about 293-309 s) or Spark 4.2.0 (532-702 s depending on the S3A stream). ETL on loopback is close among the fast engines: Polars 19.704 s, Sail without reorder 19.596 s, DuckDB 23.149 s.

SF10 was run with the same protocol for Polars, plus a same-session DuckDB reference (the original Phase 2 matrix was SF30 only, so there is no like-for-like Spark, Sail or Comet SF10 comparison here). On local files the SF10 TPC-H medians were 21.792 s for Polars and 12.718 s for DuckDB. At 10 ms latency Polars was again faster: 17.801 s versus DuckDB 29.366 s. The first Polars SF10 loopback TPC-H rep (55.728 s) started immediately after an 11.29 GiB MinIO upload and is a cold-cache outlier; the second rep was 15.956 s. That median of 35.842 s should not be read on its own.

## Correctness

All 22 TPC-H queries matched DuckDB on both `hash_set` and `hash_ordered`, at SF10 and SF30, on every storage condition and both reps. SQL features used in the ETL set (`GROUP BY ALL`, `QUALIFY`, `ANTI JOIN`, `UNION ALL BY NAME`) were accepted. ETL steps E1-E5 matched DuckDB row counts and order-free digests at both scales (E3 at SF30 is 2,999,930 rows; E5 is 6,828,731). E6 matched keys, statuses and row counts (2,275,575 at SF10; 6,828,731 at SF30) but not the digest.

## The E6 half-cent difference

E6 updates 1995 orders where `o_orderkey % 10 = 0` with `CAST(o_totalprice * 1.1 AS DECIMAL(15,2))`, then anti-joins and unions the rest. A probe of that CAST on the source tables found 22,812 half-cent ties at SF10 and 68,330 at SF30. DuckDB rounds those ties half-up; Polars SQL CAST rounds them half-even (banker's rounding). Where the two modes disagree, Polars is always 0.01 lower, and the Polars result is even in every tie (22,812 of 22,812 at SF10; 68,330 of 68,330 at SF30). The disagreeing counts are 11,416 rows at SF10 and 34,153 at SF30. The remaining ties agree, because half-up and half-even pick the same side when that side is already even. Example at SF10: 198194.75 * 1.1 = 218014.225 becomes 218014.23 in DuckDB and 218014.22 in Polars. Example at SF30: 293975.35 * 1.1 = 323372.885 becomes 323372.89 and 323372.88. Non-tie values match. This is an engine rounding-mode difference, not a broken join or union, and it is the same family of DECIMAL half-cent disagreement the original study already treated as a canary rather than a benchmark defect.

## Memory

Every job ran in the 9 GiB cgroup with swap disabled. No job was OOM-killed, no cgroup `oom` or `oom_kill` counter moved, and `POLARS_OOC_SPILL_DIR` was empty afterwards. The trustworthy figure is process-tree RSS: the Polars peak was 2.921 GB at SF10 and 6.486 GB at SF30 (ETL; TPC-H peaked at 4.931 GB). That is inside the cap with headroom. The cgroup `memory.peak` file read 9664139264 bytes on all 36 runs, including DuckDB, so it was not reset and is not usable here. Because Polars never approached the cap and never spilled, this run does not show whether Polars reads `memory.max` itself. It only shows that Polars finished SF30 TPC-H and ETL under the 9 GiB cap without OOM.
